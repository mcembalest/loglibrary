Dimension is the logarithm of capacity

How many distinguishable addresses fit in d dimensions? Insist on perfect right angles and you get only d of them. Tolerate a little interference and the count turns exponential — so the dimension you need is the logarithm of the capacity you want. Drag the tolerance; flip the axis.

Near-orthogonal capacity ≈ exp(ε²d/2) — the count of unit vectors you can pack with pairwise overlap under ε, the Johnson–Lindenstrauss regime. Orthogonal capacity is exactly d. Schematic constants; the shape is the point. On the linear axis the exponential flies off the top of the frame — on the log axis it lies down into a straight line whose slope is the exchange rate. That taming is what a logarithm is.
