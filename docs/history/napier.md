# Napier

John Napier introduced logarithms in *Mirifici Logarithmorum Canonis Descriptio* (1614). The name joins Greek *logos* (ratio, reckoning) and *arithmos* (number).

Napier defined the logarithm kinematically: two points move along lines, one at constant speed, the other at a speed proportional to its remaining distance to an endpoint. The logarithm relates the position of one point to the position of the other, mapping a geometric (multiplicative) progression onto an arithmetic (additive) one — see [from products to sums](../foundations/product-to-sum.md).
