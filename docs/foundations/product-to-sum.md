# From products to sums

The defining property of the logarithm:

$$ \log_b(xy) = \log_b(x) + \log_b(y). $$

Up to a constant factor, $\log$ is the only continuous function satisfying $f(xy) = f(x) + f(y)$ — the unique map carrying multiplication to addition (a homomorphism from the positive reals under multiplication to the reals under addition).

Consequences:

$$ \log_b\!\left(\tfrac{x}{y}\right) = \log_b x - \log_b y, \qquad \log_b(x^k) = k\,\log_b x, \qquad \log_b x = \frac{\log_a x}{\log_a b}. $$
