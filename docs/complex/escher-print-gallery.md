# The Escher print gallery

M. C. Escher's lithograph *Prentententoonstelling* ("Print Gallery," 1956) shows a man looking at a print of a harbour town that contains the gallery he is standing in — a self-referential loop with a blank patch at its centre that Escher left unfilled.

B. de Smit and H. W. Lenstra showed that the image is built from a straight, self-similar (Droste) picture warped by a conformal map. The warping is generated by the complex exponential and its inverse, the complex logarithm: in log-space the picture's self-similarity is a simple repetition, and exponentiating back turns an endless zoom into a closed loop. The Droste scaling factor is 256.
