# Complex logarithms

For a nonzero complex number $z = re^{i\theta}$, the logarithm is

$$ \log z = \ln r + i(\theta + 2\pi k), \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}. $$

It is **multivalued**: every nonzero $z$ has infinitely many logarithms, differing by integer multiples of $2\pi i$. The principal value $\operatorname{Log} z = \ln r + i\theta$ takes $\theta \in (-\pi, \pi]$, with a branch cut along the negative real axis. The full logarithm lives on a Riemann surface — an infinite spiral over the plane.

The complex logarithm turns multiplication (including rotation and scaling) into addition, just as the real one does — the basis for the [Escher print gallery](escher-print-gallery.md).
